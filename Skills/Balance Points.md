In [[Contact Juggling]] we can think of the movement of the sphere in two modes.  

In the first, we have control over the sphere at some point of our body.  Say, we're holding the sphere in our palm.  We can move that sphere around happily by simple moving it.  There are many applications of this kind of movement, but simultaneously it is trivial.  It is likely not enough for a complete and interesting performance.

The alternative involves balancing the ball at some point on our body and then manipulating the ball around the body from balance point to balance point.  

What is a balance point?  In theory, any point on the body is one.  With precision and skill you can balance the sphere anywhere.  However, some points are easier than others.  

A valley between at least two hills is usually your best bet.  In the center of the palm, the entire hand scoops down. So this is the amongst the easiest balance points.  In every direct, the gradient pushes the ball towards the stable balance point. 

There are then other points such as between the second and third knuckles on the fingers where the hand can form a valley between two hills.  These points are also very stable.

At the base of the palm there is another one.  This is harder.  There is a valley between the mounts on the left and right sides of the palm.  However, there is also a hill which leads down the wrist or down in to the base of the palm.  

Much of the time practicing [[Contact Juggling]] will involve learning new balance points and becoming comfortable rolling the ball from one balance point to the other.  Basically every trick boils down to this movement.
